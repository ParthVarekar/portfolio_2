The Texture of Matter

The first principle of our inquiry must be this: nothing is ever created out of nothing by divine will, and nothing that exists is annihilated entirely.

Once we have learned that nothing comes from nothing, we shall then have a clearer view of what we are looking for — both the source from which each thing can be created and the manner in which everything is done without the aid of gods.

Matter is never destroyed. Every body that seems to perish only transforms; it passes into new things and by passing nourishes them.

On the Nature of Atoms

Furthermore, nature resolves everything back into its component atoms and does not reduce things to nothing. As things are, since the atoms are of solid single nature, they can abide through the ages, though everything around them may be dissolved and reformed.

The Void and Its Properties

Again, if there were no void, the universe would be a solid mass. And so body and void are the two primary constituents of nature, neither of which can be reduced to the other.

Motion and the Falling of Atoms

The atoms fall straight down through empty space by their own weight; but at quite unpredictable times and places, they swerve ever so little from their course. Without this swerve, nature would never have created anything.

Consider the sheet of papyrus between your fingers. When you draw your thumb across it, thousands of fibres deflect, vibrate, and spring back. It is this — the mechanical negotiation of surface — that produces what we call texture.

The Infinity of the Universe

Now listen: the universe in all its parts has no boundary in any direction. Whatever place anyone occupies, he leaves the universe just as infinite in all directions.

The sum of things is such that there is no end to it in any direction. What is infinite cannot have a centre. And if it has no centre, it can have no outer edge.
